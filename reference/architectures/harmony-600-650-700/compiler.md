# What Logitech's compiler writes for the Harmony 600, 650 and 700

A Harmony 600, 650 or 700 does nothing useful until a configuration is written to it, and nobody writes
that configuration by hand. A person describes their devices and activities in Logitech's software, and
Logitech's service **compiles** the description into the file the remote runs. This page says what that
compiler puts into the file for these three models: not how the bytes are laid out, which is
[the configuration format](../../config-format/README.md), but what the compiler chooses to write.

Two kinds of thing come out of it:

* **The remote's own plumbing**, the same on every configuration of a model but for a handful of
  settings: the lists that answer the battery, the USB cable, the screen light and the keys, the
  firmware's own status screens, and the timers. A person sets none of it.
* **What follows from the setup**: for each device a set of pages listing its commands, a list of the
  devices to choose from, a menu of the activities, and for each activity a "Starting" screen, a working
  screen of commands and a map of what every key does while it runs, plus the variables that remember
  which device is on and on which input.

In short, and each of these is worked out further down:

* **Every page is the four buttons around the display.** A device's commands, the device list and the
  activity menu are all pages whose labels sit beside those four buttons, with a page counter at the top
  when there is more than one page[^s285][^s312].
* **The activity menu shows two activities a page**, each across both buttons of a row[^s289].
* **An activity's keys come from two of its devices**: the volume keys from the one that takes the
  volume, every other key from the one that changes channels or plays, each running that device's own
  command for the key[^s323].
* **Starting an activity shows "Starting" and its name**, switches the devices on and to their inputs,
  and ends on the activity's working screen[^s290].
* **A device's commands wait for the delay between devices** only inside an activity's start, All Off
  and Help, never when the device is used on its own[^s335].

Logitech's compiler runs on their service and is not in hand, so every rule here is read off its output:
first thirteen compiles of the three models[^s312], later twenty two to twenty four, two of the Harmony
600, thirteen of the Harmony 650 and seven of the Harmony 700[^s356]. Where this project's codec rebuilds
a part, the function that does it is named, since it is the rule in executable form: the rebuild is
compared byte for byte with every compile the rule was read on, and a **blind control**, every byte the
rule claims to generate overwritten with `0xEE` before the rebuild, shows that the rule produced the
result and not the input[^s356].

## The firmware's own wiring on arch 14

**The plumbing every configuration carries**: the lists, timers and key bindings that connect the
firmware to a configuration[^s347]. Built by `buildWiring` in
`packages/codec/src/families/four-slot/wiring.ts` from a description `describeWiring` reads, byte for byte
on the thirteen compiles[^s312] and on six later Harmony 650 compiles[^s347], and with the tree the lists
call on 22 compiles and on one compile with no activity[^s360], every generated byte overwritten in the
input first. Not overwritten, and so read, are the frame, which piece each table entry names, each
piece's length and each table's entry count, and the skin. `checkWiring` compares a configuration's
wiring with its own rebuild. Skins 66, 71, 72 and 73; the European 650 and 700 are not read, and no
compile shows a Harmony 700 with the Remote Assistant off[^unchecked].

| structure | generated | the description's |
|---|---|---|
| base slot 2 | `16384, 0x1E0000, 0x200000` | nothing |
| base slot 4 | code `k` to mode `N + k`, `k` 0 to 29, fallback `N`, `N` the firmware's screen count | nothing |
| base slot 15 | nine groups, the model's values | nothing |
| base slot 12 | the firmware's timers, their durations but one, kind 1 | the glow time; a device's timer whole and its index |
| base slot 8's leading list | eleven instructions, the firmware's screens | the quiet list's index |
| base slot 9 entries 0 to 4 | tags, flags 1, order, every binding's opcode | the shared lists' indices; each activity key's entry, or empty; the activity count, the entry count less six |
| the leftover entry | its four bindings and three lists, two bindings and two lists without Help | the lists, maps, `S`, `F` and Delay Fixing mode they name, and its own index, which `start` selects |
| base slot 10's front | the lists up to the clock's hour list, in order, and so their indices | what they call |
| the shared lists | `boot`, `events`, `everyKey`, `elevenKeys`, `quiet`, the timers' lists | their indices and what they call |
| the tree they call[^s360] | every list below, but the three carried | their indices, the low battery flag, the tour's mark, Off's mode, the tour's first screen where shown, and on the 700 the Assistant's variable and a location map |
| settings | | glow time, tilt sensor, Remote Assistant, the Harmony 700's boot step `3F F715`, the tour shown, the restore of saved delays, Help |

**The firmware's screens** are the first modes of base slot 6, and their count is the event map's `N`,
fourteen or nineteen[^s347]:

```
           5 Insert batteries   6 Unable to charge   7 Update Successful   8 Upgrade Successful
           9 blank   10 Ready to learn   11 Command Received   12 blank   13 Terminate Entry     N = 14
700        0 to 3 the four cycling frames, then the 600's list with Please charge your remote
           after Low Battery                                                                 N = 19
```

**Base slot 9's fixed entries** bind, entry 0 events `0x1A` to `0x1D` and entry 4 events `0x18` and
`0x19` to `events`; entry 2 the press of scans 1 to 5, 7 to 9, 11, 34 and 35 to `elevenKeys`, every other
scan to `everyKey` and, with the tilt sensor on only, event `0x17` to `[call everyKey, 3F F101]`; entry 3
every press to `everyKey`, and on the Harmony 700 `0x1E` to `07 FFFB`[^s347]. Entry 1 is per model and
holds the activity keys, [below](#arch-14s-entry-1-and-an-activitys-key-map)[^s314]. Stored in
`compilerTagOrder`'s slot order (`tagSlotOrder`) with the ties `0x17` before `0x9E`, `0x1E` before `0x97`,
`0x86` before `0x2D`, and `0xA3` before `0x81` on the Harmony 600 and 650 but `0x81` before `0xA3` on the
Harmony 700[^s347].

**The front of base slot 10**, list 0 onward: on the Harmony 700 the USB screen's leave handler; the Low
Battery screen's Exit `[07 FFFC, v:=0]`; `start`, `startUsb`, `startTour`, `startUpgraded`, `learned`;
entry 1's own lists in its stored order, an empty activity key's `[07 FFFD, 7E add an Activity]` among
them; with the tilt sensor on, entry 2's `0x17` list[^s347]. Then the clock's hour list[^s324].

**The firmware's timers**, in order, a device's timer at its carried index among them: light off, 10 s,
`[1F E900, 3F F101]` or `1F E900` alone with the tilt sensor off; on the Harmony 700 four of 2 s cycling
frames 1, 2, 3, 0; the screen light, the glow time; the tick, 2 s; the screen light again, 10 s; USB
return, 3 s, entering USB Connected. A device's timer writes `<label>_OnlinePower_2`, 10 of 13[^s347].

**Not established**: what the tick, `3F F00A`, `3F F632`, `3F F715`, `0F FFA0` and `0F FFA1` do; which
events `0x10` to `0x30` are, `0x17` read as the tilt by the settings compile alone; what orders the timer
table; what decides the boot step[^unchecked].

**The tree the generated lists call**, measured on 23 compiles, every shape the same on every compile of
a model and the Harmony 600's the 650's[^s360]. Most lists are a pair, 28 of the 40 on a Harmony 600 or
650, `[1F FB0k, call T]` and `T = [71 form|v, then, else]`[^s140]; `v` is one of the firmware's own
variables 9, 14, 15, 16 and 17, the same number on every compile, or the configuration's low battery flag
`b`, tour mark `t` or, on the Harmony 700, the Assistant's variable `a`. One armed is form `0x00`, two
armed `0x80`; `0x01` and `0x04` are other operations. Within the tree's conditional lists, 40 on a
Harmony 600 or 650 and 78 on a Harmony 700, a list is shared by its callers or repeated per call site as
each compile has it, and the split is per model; elsewhere in the wiring sharing is the rule, `everyKey`
alone being called from over a hundred sites. Shared are `stopLights`, five callers on all 23, and
`tour`, two where the Assistant is on; on the Harmony 700 also `everyKey.on`, two, and
`entry1.0x27.shared` and `entry1.0x24.shared`, four each. Repeated are `lightOn` and `lightOnAgain` on all
23; on the Harmony 700 also `cycle0` and `entry1.0x23.second`, and six comparisons of one body. Each list
has one name, so a shared list is one name several templates call and a repeated body is several names.
Timers by their table index, `1F EB00|k` start and `1F EA00|k` read as stop[^s360]:

```
  stopLights        [1F EA00|light on, 1F EA00|light again, 1F EA00|light off]
  lit               [call stopLights, 1F E90C, 1F E918]; elevenKeys's arms have no 1F E918
  again, on         lit then 1F EB00|light again, 1F EB00|light on
```

On the Harmony 700 the battery events run deeper trees of the same pairs over variables 9, 14 and `b`,
entering Low Battery and "Please charge your remote", and its USB leave handler's second call is
`k0 T 0x00|a, 72 map`; `families/four-slot/wiring.ts` holds each[^s360]. Scan 4's front list with no
activity at all, base slot 9's entry count less six being 0, is `[marker := 0, 07 FFFD, enter add
Activities]`, on the one Harmony 650 compile with no activity[^s360]; no Harmony 600 or 700 compile without
an activity has been read[^unchecked]. Nor has a compile whose activities leave all three activity keys
empty, which a root button map saved with the keys left out gives, so what the compiler binds scan 4 to
then is not known[^unchecked].

**Three lists the wiring calls are the entries to other parts of the product**, setup dependent, and
carried rather than built[^s360]:

| list | what it is | on |
|---|---|---|
| `start.reset` | `[07 FFF3, a call per saved delay, 07 FFF2]`, each call four lists reading one delay back from the settings store[^s303] | 23 of 23 |
| `assistantGate` | four lists, the Remote Assistant's branch on its variable and the tour's mark | 20 with the Assistant on |
| `allOff` | entry 1's binding of scan 6, read as All Off's, eight lists of Help's that may offer a "Fix it now" wizard, every other path selecting the idle entry | 23 of 23 |

None of the three is off in any Logitech compile[^s360], and MyHarmony's client offers only the Assistant
among them as a setting[^s360b]; what a configuration without each looks like is described in
`WiringSettings` in `families/four-slot/wiring.ts`, and is not the compiler's. Scan 6 is read as All Off
from that list, not pressed on a remote, and the [Harmony 650's keys](../../remotes/harmony-650/keys.md)
list All Off's scan as not measured[^unchecked].

## The device list and device mode pages on arch 14

**A device's own pages, the device lists and the activity menu are one kind of screen**: a page labels
the four buttons around the display, and pressing one of them does what its label says. Measured first on
four configurations of real setups, one Harmony 650, one Harmony 600 and two Harmony 700[^s285].

**A device list row is two instructions**, where the Harmony One's is three with a beep in
front[^s285]:

```
u8 2
u16 mode;   u8 0x7E        enter the device's mode
u16 1;      u8 0x80 + v    write 1 into the marker variable v, one per configuration
```

**A page labels the four buttons around the display** and binds only their scans, filled in this
order[^s285]:

| order | scan | corner | label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | top left | starts at x 3, y 40 |
| 2 | 2 | top right | ends at x 125, y 40 |
| 3 | 9 | bottom left | starts at x 3, y 90 |
| 4 | 34 | bottom right | ends at x 125, y 90 |

The y is a one line label's in any font. A two line label starts at 25 or 75, its second line one font
height below in the same font, which is 40 or 90 in the usual label fonts and 35 to 39 in smaller ones.
Every one of the 184 device list and device mode pages binds a prefix of that order, 414 of 414 right hand
labels on their corner pages end at 125 and 448 of 448 left hand ones start at 3, and every page holds four
but the last of its mode and one page of each 700's receiver mode. **A page stores its entries in another
order**, 9, 8, 34, 2 with an unbound scan left out, 184 of 184. **Every row list is bound once**: 300 of
300, none shared between two buttons or between a page and its copy[^s285].

### A corner page's chrome

**A corner page carries its own chrome**, with no call to a shared program[^s285]:

```
op 2    0, 0, background       one picture for a page of one item or none, another for more, 173 of 173
op 17   queue 0x73 operand 1       173 of 184, absent on the 11 two row list pages
op 3    the top bar
op 16   title font;    op 4 or 5 at (0, 2), the title
op 16   counter font;  three texts at y 2: n / m, on a mode of several pages only, at x 0x6A, 0x71, 0x76
                       for two to nine pages, 98 of 98; 0x63, 0x6A, 0x6F on a two row list page
op 16   label font;    the labels
op 3    the bottom bar
op 16;  op 4 or 5      the bottom bar's word: Back on a device mode, Activity or Activities on a list
op 0
```

A mode of ten pages or more draws the counter further left, `0x63` and then `0x5C` from page 10. A mode's
block is its own tagged list, then per page its program and a **six** byte page record, `u24 list; u24
program`, since these models' pages carry no lead byte, then the entry[^s285].

**A device list's paging is its counter and nothing else**[^s312]: no counter on 7 of 7 one page corner
lists, all of them the Harmony 600's, `n/m` on 135 of 135 pages of the 59 multi page lists, and a corner
list's record list `0x72` under tag `0x99` and `0x73` under tag `0x2D` at one page and at several, 53 of
53, so no page turn binding changes with the page count. A corner page holding one item draws the device
mode pages' one item background, 7 of 7, and every two row page after the first its list's first page's
picture, 30 of 30. A page added to a full list holds the new item in its first place, and a list of one
page gains the counter right after its title[^s312][^s334].

**A device mode page's chrome**, on the 83 device modes and 639 pages of the thirteen compiles;
`deviceModeChrome` builds it and checks every device mode against what it builds[^s330]:

| what | rule | agree |
|---|---|---|
| background | `OP_IMAGE` at 0, 0: the look's single picture for a page of one item or none, its crossed one for more | 639 of 639 |
| look | by skin and content key: 72 and 66 the colour look of 2026, 66 that of 2021 and 2023, 71 and 73 the monochrome; the one whose five pictures the configuration holds; 69, 74 and 75 not read | 13 of 13 |
| queued program | `0x11 lo hi 0x73`, base slot 11's first program no base slot 14 case names that opens with a one byte switch on variable 17, the sending flag[^s369]; it draws the look's corner patch | 639 of 639 |
| bars | `DRAW_IMAGE_AT 0,0,0,0,128,16` the top bar after the queue; `DRAW_IMAGE_AT 0,0,0,0,128,128` the bottom bar before the word | 639 of 639 |
| bottom word | font 1, the first set of the title's size; "Back" at `floor((128 - width) / 2)` = 49, y 114; drawn inline once per configuration, every other page `TEXT_AT` pointing at that copy | 639 of 639 |

**The corner program**, which draws the sending mark, is one shape on every Harmony 600, 650 and 700 compile,
70 of 70 on 24, three runs back to back[^s374][^s369]:

```
+0x00  12 11 01 00 u24 case0  01 01 03 u24 range   switch on variable 17: case 0, range 1 to 3
+0x0D  11 FA FF 07 00                              the tail: queue 0x07 0xFFFA, end
+0x12  02 72 70 u24 patch  14 u24 tail             case 0: the state's 12 by 10 patch at 114, 112
+0x1C  02 72 70 u24 mark   14 u24 tail             range: the mark at 114, 112
```

What `0x07 0xFFFA` does is not read[^unchecked].

**Which corner program a mode queues follows what is running**, measured on the Harmony 650's thirteen
compiles[^s366]. Three programs, differing in the patch they draw:

| program's patch | queued by | modes over the thirteen |
|---|---|---|
| grey | every device mode, every device list but the two row one, the idle device list | 87, 53 and 13 |
| red | the activity menu and every screen of an activity | 13 and 58 |
| plain | every other screen: Help's, the Remote Assistant's, the delay settings, the setup prompts | 2828 |
| none | the two row device list, from neither its key map nor its pages | 13, one per compile |

A page's own program queues its mode's corner program again, so the program is named on every page and not
only in the mode's key map[^s366].

**Logitech's background pictures do not follow those states.** Three of the five designed backgrounds are
shared across them: the idle device list draws the device pages' crossed picture and their one item picture,
and the activity menu's page of one row draws the same picture as an activity's page of one command. Of the
934 pages drawing a background, the 813 device pages are on the grey look and the 67 activity pages on the
red, while the 54 idle pages are split, 25 grey and 29 red, so nothing in Logitech's pictures marks the idle
state[^s366].

### A device mode's key map

**A device mode's own list binds every key**: 47 entries on all 21 device modes of the four
configurations, the four corner scans bound to nothing, one `0x72` and one `0x73`, and every other key an
action list or nothing, 547 of 547 bound keys sending that device's command[^s285]. On the thirteen
compiles' 83 device modes `deviceModeKeyMap` builds it[^s330]:

| what | rule | agree |
|---|---|---|
| key map tags | presses of the 41 `HARD_KEYS` scans, the four corners and scan 25 (`0x99`), and `0x2D`: 47 | 83 of 83 |
| key map order | `compilerTagOrder`: the case hash masked to 16 slots doubled while over three quarters full, 64 here; `0x2D` before `0xA4`, the one shared slot | 83 of 83 |
| fixed entries | `0x99` `0x72` on `(record << 8) \| counter`, the record the activity menu names, one of two with the same keys and byte identical targets; `0x2D` `0x73` the queued program; corners nothing; the rest `0x7F` or nothing | 83 of 83 |

The slot order holds on all 6921 mode and page lists of two tags or more in the thirteen. The order
inside a shared slot is insertion order and not the tag's, `0xC3` and `0x87` being stored both ways, so
only the device mode's measured pair is answered[^s330]. Which of the two records is named follows no
index, lower on seven compiles and higher on the six power hold ones, so it is read off the activity
menu[^s330].

### The two row list

**One device list per configuration is laid out in two rows instead of four corners**: a device per row
bound to both of that row's scans, 2 and 8 or 9 and 34, each binding running a row list of its own, its
label at y 35 or 79 and `x = floor((128 - width) / 2)`, 21 of 21. Its pages all draw one picture, the
corner lists' crossed one on the 650 and 700s and one of its own on the 600, and carry no queued
`0x73`[^s285]. Its counter sits at `0x63`, `0x6A`, `0x6F`, ending at 118[^s334].

**Nothing enters it**, on the thirteen compiles[^s326]: no `0x7E` anywhere in the container names its
mode, counted as the bytes `lo hi 0x7E` over the whole file, 0 on every compile against at least one for
every corner list and every row menu, 232 lists; no event map value is it; no page or program is shared
with another mode; and no configuration instruction is a run time builder, `0x7B` or an opcode `0x1F` to
`0x3E` with operand high byte `0xF7`. The firmware's mode switch has four direct callers and takes its
number only from an `0x7E` operand, the event map's value or fallback, or the mode stack, on all five
application builds of these models; a host can also queue an `0x7E` through command state `0x34` or write
the mode bytes through `0xB1`, neither a route for a person[^s326b]. Its mode number differs per compile,
from 166 to 337, and its record list is `0x72` under tag `0x99`, the corner lists' centre key
operand[^s326]. So a page in it is not visible on a remote; the corner lists, four to a page, are the
device lists a person reaches, along with the Help key's "Select device" picker, whose rows run `0x7E`
directly and which is not counted as a corner list[^s326].

## The delay step before every command

**Every command list opens with a `0x7F` call**, 422, 188 and 372 on one Harmony 650, 600 and 700
configuration each: `0x1F` loading 1, then a list testing a variable against it with `0x71` and, while it
matches, calling a list of the device's own that is one `0x72` on that device's `InterDeviceDelay` variable
through a base slot 14 table. The variable is 52, 46 and 59, and every list writing it sets it to 1 and
back to 0 inside itself, 31, 27 and 50[^s287][^s335b].

**Both operands are constants around the start variable**, over the thirteen compiles, 6100 send lists of
6100 opening with the prelude: `load` is `0xFB01` on all of them, and `condition` is `0x0000 | S`, equality
and one arm, `S` being the variable every activity's enter list sets to 1 as its second step, after the
start up screen, and to 0 as its last, 40 activities of 40, one `S` per configuration[^s319].
`SEND_PRELUDE_LOAD` and `sendPreludeCondition`.

**Those writers are, and are only**, each reached by one tagged list entry[^s335b]: an activity's handler
lists, tag 1 (its start), tag 2 (left) and tag 5 (selected again); the Off key map's tags 1 and 5, which is
All Off, whose `0x72` queueing the all off list sits between raising the variable and lowering it; and a
mode's enter handler, tag 6, on 16 of the 69 screens drawing "Exit Help" on the 650 and on every
"Attempting to fix" screen. A leave list, tag 2, is the bare pair and sends nothing. No device mode record,
page key, call, transition or queued instruction names one. So the delay between devices applies in an
activity's start, in All Off and in Help, which a Help key pressed in device mode reaches through the Off
key map[^s333], and not to a device's own command pressed in device mode:

| writers of the start variable | a Harmony 650 | a Harmony 600 | another 600 | a Harmony 700 | another 650 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| activity, tags 1, 2 and 5 | 3, 3, 3 | 3, 3, 3 | 2, 2, 2 | 5, 5, 5 | 6, 4, 6 |
| the Off key map, tags 1 and 5 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Help question, tag 6 | 16 | 12 | 12 | 27 | 22 |
| "Attempting to fix", tag 6 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 6 | 5 |

**Each table case is read**[^s287]: 21 cases, 0 to 20 tenths, each a screen program queueing `0x67` with
`(group << 8) | value` for the device's own group, so tables differ per device in that byte only, stored in
the order 0 to 15, 17, 16, 19, 18, 20. `load` and `condition` are private to each command, `delay` shared
by the device, 1598 of 1598.

**What the queued entry does is measured on a Harmony 650, and not read in the firmware**[^s335]: the value
holds back the device's **own** command by a tenth of a second per unit, 15 units lengthening the silence
before that device by 1.506 s in a start and 1.505 s in All Off, while the same change to the device sent
before it left the start's silence at 0.620 s. The wait begins once the device sent before it has gone, so
it does not count down alongside the other devices' waits as a per device quantity would, if the list's
instructions all run before its first code goes out, which is not measured. Whether it holds back a device
queued after it is not measured, and neither is the Harmony 600 or 700[^unchecked]. `sendPreludes` and
`interDeviceDelayCases`; `composeDevice` emits all of it on these models.

**A delay saved on the remote** is read back over the configuration's own at every start, by the restore
list `start.reset` above[^s303].

## Arch 14's entry 1 and an activity's key map

**On these models the prefix and the left over entry are generated**, entries 0 to 4 and the leftover
entry's bindings, order and lists, 13 of 13[^s347]; [the firmware's own wiring](#the-firmwares-own-wiring-on-arch-14)
above has them.

**Entry 1 holds the activity keys** (Harmony 600, 650 and 700)[^s314]. It is a prefix entry nothing
selects, and its press entries for the three activity keys are one of two four byte forms in all 13
Logitech compiles measured:

| key | scan | press entry |
|---|---|---|
| Watch TV | 5 | `80+scan`, `FF00 + n`, `1F`: select entry `n`, an activity's key map |
| Watch a Movie | 1 | the same |
| Listen to Music | 7 | the same, or `80+scan`, `p`, `7F`: call list `p` = `[07 FFFD, 7E m]`, push the mode and enter the "add an Activity" mode `m` (mode 0 on the 600 and 650, mode 4 on the 700) |

Both forms are the same length, so putting an activity on a key or emptying one is a same length edit:
`setActivityKey` and `clearActivityKey` in `packages/codec/src/families/four-slot/activitykeys.ts`, and
`composeActivity`'s `activityKey`. **Emptying needs list `p` to exist already**: Logitech compiles it only
while some key is empty, 6 of the 13, which are two setups[^s314], and adding it is a length change.
**Appending the list first works**: `appendActionLists` with `[07 FFFD, 7E m]` and then
`clearActivityKey`, written to a Harmony 650 whose compile had all three keys taken, opened the "add an
Activity" screen[^s341]. The Harmony One is not compared[^unchecked].

**Which activity a key gets is not its type alone**[^s340]: an activity saved again as `Custom`, its group
read back as 0, kept the Listen to Music key in Logitech's compile, and an activity `Custom` from the start
held none. Over fifteen compiles a key is empty exactly where the activities number two, Listen to Music
each time. MyHarmony assigns the three keys in a button map of their own, its "Customize Activity Buttons"
screen, which offers no empty choice[^s340b].

### A device kept on between activities

**A device kept on between activities**, MyHarmony's per device power setting
(`IsPoweredOnBetweenActivities`)[^s340b], has its write of 0 left out of the start of every activity that
does not use it, and nothing else changes: it is still switched on by the activities that use it and still
in the All Off list[^s340]. Measured on one device in one compile pair on the Harmony 650. `keepDeviceOn`
applies it to the activities a configuration holds, and `activityPowerTargets`'s `keepOn` to an activity
composed after; applied to the compile without the setting, the starts equal the compile with it.

### An activity's key map

**An activity's key map is built from two roles** (Harmony 600, 650 and 700), measured on the 40
activities of the 13 Logitech compiles[^s323]:

| keys | press entry runs |
|---|---|
| VolumeUp, VolumeDown, Mute (scans 14, 15, 16) | the **volume** device's own device mode list for that scan |
| every other scan | the **control** device's own device mode list for that scan: the channel changing device, else the one that plays |
| UpArrow, DownArrow (26, 27), unbound in the control device's own map | its DirectionUp, DirectionDown lists (50, 42); right on 5 distinct activities and wrong on 4 in the 13 compiles, and right on all 3 it fires on in two contributed Harmony 650 configurations |
| ChannelUp, ChannelDown (31, 32), where its own map runs the DirectionUp and DirectionDown lists there | its SkipForward, SkipBack lists (38, 21); fires on one activity, a disc player's, and agrees with Logitech's stated map there |
| Exit (12), where the control device's own screen has an item labelled `Exit` | that item's list; the same one activity |

The **same base slot 10 list** as the device's own map, never a copy: 1254 of the 1328 activity key
bindings, and none of the other 74 runs a list with the same codes. The result is Logitech's set key for
key on 20 of the 40 activities, about 14 distinct; the other 20 are nine copies of one activity, four keys
each, and 11 activities of three configurations customised by hand. On two Harmony 650 configurations set
up by other owners it is Logitech's on 4 of 6 activities and 223 of 226 keys, the three others on the one
activity each that a user may have edited[^s323]. A display device that neither changes channels nor plays
contributes no key. Scans 26 and 27 are `UpArrow` and `DownArrow` and 50 and 42 the pad's, decided from the
device maps, [the 600's keys](../../remotes/harmony-600/keys.md). `activityKeysFromRoles` in
`packages/codec/src/activityroles.ts`. **Help, scan 3, is the one key the roles do not give**: every
activity's map binds its release to the activity's own "Attempting to fix the problem" mode and its repeat
to `[07 FFFD, 7E <Delay Fixing>]`, 40 of 40, while no device mode binds scan 3 at all, 0 of 83, so Help
is the running activity's own binding and not a device's[^s333].

**A pass through device**, one in an activity that MyHarmony gives no role, an HDMI switch the picture only
passes through, is switched on in the activity's start and set to its input, and bound no key and no screen
item[^s339]. Measured on one Logitech compile with the role stated, a Harmony 650's; a contributed Harmony
700 configuration has the same shape with the role inferred. Nothing in a configuration states the role: a
display device with no key and no screen item looks the same. `activityFromRoles` in
`packages/codec/src/activityroles.ts`.

Read with `gspm.handler_sets` and `gspm.handler_index`[^s39].

## The screen sections by category, arch 14

**Screen bytes** are the bytes the byte accounting gives base slots 6, 7 and 11, the picture bank and base
slot 17's two bytes on these models (`SCREEN_OWNERS`). Base slot 14 is excluded. Each is in exactly one
**unit**, the thing a generator emits whole, and each unit is one of three categories[^s317]:

| category | means | units on a Harmony 650 |
|---|---|---|
| fixed | emitted for every setup, content independent of it | the 44 head modes of the mode table (7 binding a keypad, 37 binding nothing), the 10 tour modes, Off, 15 of the 19 pictures, the glyphs those and the dynamic programs draw where their count agrees across compiles, the font set holding only such glyphs, base slot 17's two bytes |
| dynamic | draws state the remote holds at run time, by switching on a state variable | the 5 base slot 11 entries no value map names (three icons switching on state variable 17, the sending flag[^s369], and the two delay countdowns) and the 4 pictures only they draw |
| setup | exists, repeats or holds content because of the devices, activities and names | device modes, device lists, start up screens, help, Remote Assistant, the activity menu and working screens, every case program (keyed by a device's delay or the running activity, or refused by the census), glyphs only setup screens draw and the few whose count varies, the other font set headers, the three tables whose lengths count modes, programs or fonts |

**Fixed means the same look**: addresses, glyph codes, font indices, the operands of instructions that
index a numbered table, which variable a state write writes, and the number of a switched state variable
above 17 are placement and abstracted away. On the Harmony 650, 40 of the 55 fixed modes are also byte
identical once relocated and 15 have to be re-encoded with the configuration's own numbers[^s317].

On four Harmony 650 compiles the screen bytes run from 628447 to 681067: fixed 471328 or 471329, dynamic
13391 on each, and the setup's 143727 to 196348[^s317]. Fixed is per model and per service generation: 53 of
the 55 fixed modes and all 19 pictures recur on the 2026 Harmony 700 compiles, none of the modes and 2 of
the pictures on the Harmony 600's[^s317]. Read with `screenUnits`, `attributeScreens` and
`screenCategoryTotals`.

**The Harmony 650's pictures**, nineteen across its 13 compiles, 18 or 19 in each, every one byte identical
wherever held; `HARMONY_650_PICTURES` names each by its content key[^s363].

| kind | pictures | bytes |
|---|---|---|
| simple enough to state as a rule, built by `buildPictures` | the plain background (fill `0x2945`, 128 by 128), the top bar (16 rows, one colour each), the bottom bar (16 rows from row 112, inset 22, 20, 19, 18, 17, 17, then 16, end pixels in their own colour on rows 113 to 119), three corner patches (fills `0x2945` 12 by 12, `0xB944` and `0x4A49` 12 by 10) | 41066 |
| designed artwork | device page crossed and of one item, activity menu of two and of one, working screen crossed, start up picture, corner mark, and the four firmware screen pictures of USB Connected, Low Battery, the battery blank screen and the upgrade and learn blank screens | 327891 where all are held |
| drawn only by the delay screens Help opens | the black delay picker with a dotted cross and the black delay screen with a dotted line | 65546 |

The six rule pictures are also Logitech's bytes on the five Harmony 700 compiles of 2026; the top bar and
the red patch on all seven Harmony 700 compiles, and the top bar on both Harmony 600 compiles[^s363].

## A device's inputs, and the state tables built

How the compiler wires a device's power, its on and off sends and its three delays, is
[sending](../../infrared/sending.md#how-the-compiler-wires-a-devices-power).

**A device's inputs**, measured on the thirteen compiles[^s321], and the same on the Harmony One but for
the send, which is two instructions there, [its compiler page](../harmony-one/compiler.md)[^s393]. Two
layers of one byte variables, each a record with `first` 0 and `second` the number of values less one:

| variable | values | transitions |
|---|---|---|
| `<label>_Input_<n>` | the catalogue's `inputs.list`, numbered by Logitech's `InputOrder` | one per value from `-2` (0xFFFE), or, where the inputs step with `next`, one per ordered pair of distinct values |
| `<label>_<state>_<n>` | one of the catalogue's `states`, its values in order | a value with a `select` gets one transition, from `-2` for `setType` 1 and `-3` (0xFFFD) for `setType` 2; a state with `next` gets one per ordered pair |

The catalogue's fields named here are Logitech's[^s321b].

* A transition `i` to `j` of a stepping variable runs the one step body `(j - i) mod n` times, so it steps
  forward and wraps: 128 of 128 stepping transitions[^s321].
* A transition's instruction: the null instruction (opcode 0, operand 0) for an input with no commands, 6;
  an inline state write `0x80 | variable` for an input whose only step writes one state, 103; otherwise
  `0x7F` to a list, 340 inputs and every one of the 243 state transitions. A body that is one send runs the
  send list itself; a longer one is a list of `0x7F` send list calls, bare `0x7C` waits and state
  writes[^s321].
* A send's `0x7C` quantity: an input's own sends and its `next` wait the input delay in tenths, or the key
  delay where the input delay is 0; a state's sends wait the key delay[^s321].
* A write Logitech's catalogue marks as recording rather than setting (`DevActionType` 1)[^s321b] is a call
  to a two instruction list, `[0x07 0xFFFF, 0x80 | variable]`[^s321].
* One input that steps gives a variable of one value and no transitions; one input that does not step
  gives none. A state no input writes, directly or through another state, is not compiled[^s321].
* **The catalogue's `power.onReset`** becomes one list per state, `[0x07 0xFFFF, 0x80 | variable value]`,
  called from the power variable's `0 to 1` list after the power on delay, in the catalogue's order
  (on [the Harmony One](../harmony-one/compiler.md#state-tables-and-a-devices-inputs) the same shape): 13 of 13 lists on
  the eight device instances that compose, and a fourteenth on a device that does not compose has the same
  place and flag[^s332]. The value is the named value's index, told apart from "always the first value" by
  one catalogue entry only; where the catalogue names a value the variable does not declare, twice, the
  compile writes 0, **unexplained**: one of the two is a state of one value and the other names index 0 of
  another state[^s332]. Without it a `-3` variable keeps its value across a power cycle and a second start
  asking for the same value sends nothing[^s332b].
* An activity's enter list writes power on, then the inputs in the order the devices were switched on,
  then power off, then the counter: 40 of 40 activities, 72 input writes[^s321].
* **Unexplained**: four `-3` transitions whose only step is a `Select` press are absent from one
  television's compile, a Panasonic TH-42PA30; a Panasonic TX-P42GT30E of one Harmony 700 compile holds
  `InputType` and no `Input`; and the Chromecast of one Harmony 600 compile holds a one value input variable
  and is not identified[^s321].

`inputPlan`, `composeDeviceInputs`, `activityStartTargets` and `deviceStateMachines` in
`packages/codec/src/inputs.ts`; `packages/codec/test/inputs.test.ts`.

**The state tables, built from a description**[^s324]. `buildStateTables` in
`packages/codec/src/statetables.ts`, with the four slot family's facts of
`packages/codec/src/families/four-slot/statetables.ts`, rebuilds all thirteen compiles' base slots 0, 13
and 14 byte for byte. What it generates rather than carries:

| what | value on these models |
|---|---|
| records 7 to 17, `first / max` | `0/2, 0/3, 5/7, 0/7, 0/32, 0/1, 0/32, 1/1, 0/3, 0/1, 0/3`, no transitions |
| the clock's transitions | minute, day, month: `{-2 to 0, 0x1F 0xF2nn}` incrementing hour, month, year; hour: `{-2 to 0, 0x7F list}`, the list `[0x1F 0xF203, 0x1F 0xF204]` incrementing day and weekday, its index the configuration's |
| index `narrow` | `first` 0, max 65277, no transitions, unnamed, stored straight after record 17; purpose **not read** on these models ([on the Harmony One](../harmony-one/compiler.md#state-tables-and-a-devices-inputs) it is the clock pickers' variable) |
| a variable's width | one byte for a maximum up to 100, two from 254; **between is not pinned** |
| a device's eight delay variables | `PowerOnDelay` and `InterDeviceDelay` max 65277, their `Default` copies max 254, all two bytes; `PowerOnDelayFlagCounter` 0/5, `InterDeviceDelayFlagCounter` 0/3, both `FixingTriggered` 0/100, one byte; 83 of 83 devices, `deviceDelayVariables` |
| `CurrentActivityState_0` and `CurrentLocation` | the activity count for both value and max, and 0/0, `activityStateVariables` |
| the start variable and the flag | 0/1 each, unnamed, no transitions, `startSequenceVariables`; their indices are the description's[^s329] |
| the menu marker | 0/3, unnamed, no transitions, `menuMarkerVariable`; its index is the description's[^s329] |

Carried: which variables there are, their stems, values, maxima and transitions in stored order, and the
order of their indices within each width, which no hash of their names fits[^s324]. **A value can exceed
its maximum**: `DefaultPowerOnDelay` holds 65535 under 254 for one device of one Harmony 650 compile, so
"at most `second`" holds for the populations it was first measured on[^s86][^s120] and not
for every compile[^s324]. The constants are this architecture's: on the Harmony 880 and 885 records 7 to 12
and 15 to 17 hold the same values while 13 and 14 hold `1/1, 0/1`, the Harmony 525 differs from record 8 on,
and on both, as on [the Harmony One](../harmony-one/compiler.md#state-tables-and-a-devices-inputs), the `0/65277` variable at index `narrow` is the only two byte one
while one byte maxima reach 121, inside the gap not pinned here[^s324].

Read with `gspm.state_table`, `gspm.state_records` and `gspm.state_index`; `stateTable` and
`stateRecords` in `packages/codec`[^s35][^s60][^s86].
