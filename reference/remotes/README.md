# Remotes

Every Harmony model Logitech made, and what each one's hardware can do.

A remote says which model it is by a number, its **skin**. Logitech gave every model a skin, and a model
sold under two regional names has two: the Harmony One is skin 54 and its European version skin 59. The
remote reports its skin when a host asks for its version, and the skin names the model[^s57][^s131].
**A configuration cannot say which model it is for.** It states its architecture, the platform it was
built for[^s182], and one architecture covers models that differ in what a person sees: the Harmony 600
and the Harmony 650 are both architecture 14, one with a monochrome screen and one with a colour
screen[^s387]. So anything that draws a screen or limits a setup needs the model, and this page is the
table that turns a skin into one.

**Where each column comes from**, which matters more than in most tables here, because most columns are
not Logitech's:

| column | source |
|---|---|
| skin, model, regional twin | Logitech's classic client names a model per skin[^s207]; its later service names the rest, the European skins among them[^s131]. The skin of six models is confirmed by a remote or by its firmware: 15, 22, 54, 66, 71 and 72[^s57][^s81] |
| architecture | Logitech's client places each skin on a platform[^s207], and its teaching pictures name an architecture for the older skins[^s208]; the architecture of the Harmony 525, One, 600, 650 and 700 is also read off a remote or a configuration[^s57][^s182] |
| max devices | Logitech's service, its `MaxDevicesPerAccount`[^s136] |
| favourite channel buttons | Logitech's service for the skins of the Harmony One, 300, 600, 650 and 700[^t-product-table], the comparison table for the rest[^x-forum] |
| panel, touch, radio channels, sequences, page button, sound and picture keys | the comparison table at harmony-remote-forum.de, a community site[^x-forum] |
| newest firmware the forum table knows | the same table; a floor and out of date, since later firmware exists for the Harmony 700[^s297] |

**The comparison table is a third party's**, a private project whose own terms give no warranty of
currency or correctness[^x-forum]. So its columns are hypotheses, good enough to decide what to build and
never the ground for refusing an edit. Its "Eur#" column is what pairs two regional names into one
remote: the 520 with the 525, the 510 with the 515, the 550 with the 555, the 720 with the 785, the 880 with
the 885 and the 890 with the 895[^x-forum].

**A skin names a keypad, not only a region.** The two remotes of a pair share a specification and differ
on the face: the 525 and the 885 carry four teletext colour keys their 520 and 880 twins do not. Teletext
is European, which is the mechanism, and it is why a drawing belongs to a skin rather than to a
model[^x-forum]. [The 525's README](harmony-525/README.md) and [the 885's](harmony-885/README.md) give
each face.

A skin is here when Logitech names it and this library will not describe its hardware: the rebadged
remotes, the Harmony 200, 300 and 800, the Harmony 1000 and 1100 family, and the later generation from the
Harmony Touch on[^s131]. A record needs an architecture and a panel, and inventing either would turn a gap
into a plausible wrong answer for a remote somebody owns. **The Harmony 300 and 350 have folders and no
record**: their architecture is read off a unit[^s264], and their capabilities are Logitech's service's,
stated on their features pages, rather than fields of a record.

**A remote's descriptor does not always carry its own skin.** A Harmony 300 reports skin 78 in its USB
descriptor while its own system file and its configuration state 79, the European model: the descriptor
carries the family's base skin[^s264].

## Where the skin numbers come from

Logitech's classic desktop client names 46 skins, and every number this project has measured agrees with
it[^s207]. It predates Logitech's later service, so it lacks every later model and every European variant,
and two of the numbers it lacks, 59 and 73, are the Harmony One EMEA and the Harmony 600 EMEA[^s131].
Logitech's service lists 80 skins below 100 and pairs 14 base names with a regional variant each, every
European skin allocated directly above the run of its American sibling[^s131]. The codenames the classic
client used for some models, and the names the service gives them, are
[Logitech's product line](../logitech/product-line.md#the-live-catalogue).

## What each field is worth

The columns above have the standing their source gives them, and some have been checked against the
remotes. Field by field:

| field | standing |
|---|---|
| skin and architecture | **confirmed** for skins 15, 22, 54, 66, 71 and 72, from firmware literals and from remotes, none of which consulted Logitech's table[^s57][^s81] |
| touch | **confirmed, including the negative**: base slot 17 is a touch hit map on the Harmony One's architecture and names the picture bank on every other, and the One is the only model with a record that the table calls touch[^s45][^s62] |
| panel | **Logitech's service** for the six models it was asked about[^t-capabilities]; **not derivable from a configuration**: the Harmony 600's screen is monochrome and its configuration carries two byte colour pixels like every architecture 14 configuration, so the pixel format is the architecture's and the panel the model's[^s51][^s387] |
| page button | **consistent**: the 5xx has none, and its four soft keys enter a mode by index, so paging is a soft key binding there, [the 525's keys](harmony-525/keys.md); how often its configurations bind it is not checked[^unchecked] |
| max devices | **bounded below only**: a device is an infrared group[^s86], and no configuration read reaches any stated maximum. The figures are Logitech's, from two tables that agree on 28 of 35 skins[^s136] |
| sequences | **consistent**: every architecture read runs action lists of many instructions[^s34]; the 748's "no" is not checked[^unchecked] |
| newest firmware | **a floor**: the table says 2.5.0 for the 700, and a 700 runs 2.8[^s297] |
| favourites, radio channels, sound and picture keys | the comparison table's alone, except the favourite maxima of the Harmony One, 300, 600, 650 and 700, which are Logitech's service's[^t-product-table]; **not checked** otherwise[^unchecked] |

**A device count that matches a configuration proves nothing about the maximum.** The Harmony 700's
record once said 6 because both of its configurations held six devices, and a test then asserted the
configurations sat at the maximum; a configuration holding six forbids no seventh, and Logitech's figure is
8[^s136]. A writer refusing a seventh device on a 700 on that ground would have been refusing on its own
arithmetic.

## A long press

A **long press** is a second, different action on one button, chosen by how long it is held. It is not a
repeat: whether a code repeats while a key is down is a property of the code[^s127], and a button with a
long press cannot repeat at all, because the remote has to wait to find out which of the two was meant.

Logitech's service declares it, `LongPressAction`, on 37 of its 120 product records, 17 product names, and
fifteen of those carry a skin[^t-long-press]. **Of the models described here two have one, the Harmony
350 and the Harmony Touch**[^t-long-press], and of the six models whose capabilities the service was asked
for, the Harmony 350 is the only one that states it[^t-capabilities]. Neither has a record in this
library's skin table, [above](#skins-without-a-record), which is how this page could say until plan 009's
step 4.4 that no model with a record has one and that the feature arrived with the Harmony Touch's
generation; the first half was true and the second left out the Harmony 350:

long press cannot be given a record unnoticed[^t-long-press]. On the Harmony 350 it is what makes the
device limit work: four device keys, two presses each, eight devices, where the Harmony 300 has four keys,
no long press and four devices[^t-capabilities].

## An activity limit

Logitech's product records carry a `MaxActivities` field, and it says almost nothing: it is 255 on every
disabled product, a sentinel rather than a limit, and empty on every enabled one but the Harmony 350, which
states 1[^t-product-table]. So it is not a field of the record. The Harmony 350's single activity is on its
features page.

## What Logitech's service states, model by model

For six models, Logitech's service was asked for the capabilities of one product each: the Harmony One,
600, 650, 700, 300 and 350. Each model's features page states the answer, field by field, and the
comparisons between them[^t-capabilities]. All six also state `NumOfAddDevice` 100[^t-capabilities]; what
it limits, and what enforces it, is not checked[^unchecked].

## Recognising a model and addressing it

This library recognises a reported skin on architectures 2 to 14, which is what the record is for. **It
can address a remote's flash on fewer**: only the architectures with an entry in `FLASH_TOP_BYTE_BOUND`,
which are the Harmony 525's, the One's and the Harmony 600, 650 and 700's, so a record's architecture is
not a promise that a read will be accepted[^s88]. The Harmony 900, 1000 and 1100 family, architecture 15,
enumerates as a network device rather than plain HID and has no record at all, since nothing here can reach
it; [the architectures](../architectures/README.md#three-ways-a-host-talks-to-a-remote) say why.

## Not checked

* Every capability column of a model nobody has read, which is every row without a folder[^unchecked].
* What a remote itself enforces, as against what Logitech's software allows, for devices, activities and
  favourites[^unchecked].
